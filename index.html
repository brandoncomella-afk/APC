Projectile Ring Challenge: makeup lab

Given information

  • All heights are measured from the tabletop to the center of the ring, not from the floor.
  • All horizontal distances are measured from the muzzle opening, along the bench.
  • The launcher is calibrated. Trust v₀; don’t try to measure it.

A Derive the trajectory equation 12 points · on paper

Before you touch a number, derive a general expression for the marble’s height above the tabletop as a function of horizontal distance from the muzzle. Your result must be symbolic, written entirely in terms of v₀, θ, h₀, g, and x.

Start from the two independent parametric equations of motion, one for each direction. Eliminate t and solve for y(x). Show every algebraic step. A correct answer with no derivation earns no credit.

Stuck? Open a hint

Write x(t) for the horizontal direction. There is no horizontal acceleration, so that equation solves for t in one step. Substitute that expression for t into your vertical equation. Every t disappears and you are left with y in terms of x alone.

B Compute your station’s ring height 16 points · work on paper, results here

Use your Part A result to calculate the ring height at your assigned station. Show the substitution with units on paper and report the height to the nearest 0.1 cm.

Then check it a second, independent way: find the time of flight to your station and use it to compute the height again. If the two methods disagree, find the error before you go on. You do not get a second launch.

In class, each group set one ring. You are setting all nine. Once your equation from Part A is right, you may use a calculator, Desmos, or a spreadsheet to get the other eight heights. Type each height in centimeters. Your assigned station should match your Part B answer.

D Launch 10 points

When all nine rings are set, press Launch. You get one launch. After it fires, your ring heights and Part B answers lock, and your results are scored from that one shot.

E Analysis 8 points

E1. Suppose the launcher actually fired 5% faster than the given v₀. Would the rings need to move up or down? Which station would need to move the most, and why? Use the terms in your Part A equation to explain.

E2. Find the horizontal distance where your marble reaches its highest point. Which two stations have ring heights closest to each other? Explain why, using the shape of the trajectory, even though they are at very different distances.

E3. These rings are mounted vertically, facing the launcher. Suppose a ring were mounted flat instead, like a basketball hoop. Using the marble’s velocity vector, explain why a flat ring near the apex could never work but one at station 9 might. Estimate the marble’s direction of motion at station 9 as an angle below horizontal.
